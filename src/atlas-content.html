How are material comparisons calculated?

For the same geometry, force loads, Poisson ratio and density, changing a uniform elastic modulus changes displacement but leaves the linear-elastic stress solution unchanged. Static factors scale by yield strength divided by 695 MPa; fatigue factors scale by effective fatigue amplitude divided by 277.726295 MPa. Retention ratios stay unchanged. The study uses Poisson ratio 0.3, density 7,800 kg/m³, an amplitude factor of 0.8 and the same Findley parameter interval for comparison.

These are homogeneous material calculations, not additional material-specific FE validation. Published specimen data are research examples, not production design allowables; a carburized case/core gradient, residual stress and manufacturing effects require separate evidence. The original reference retains its inherited research amplitude.

A / THE MODEL

Power-law-based
retention models.

The ligament is the material left between an opening and the tooth-root region. Opening area and ligament thickness define a geometry penalty. The reciprocal transformation converts this penalty into retained safety factor.

R̂ = 1 / (1 + P)

Exponential load mixing is used by primary static model E. Harmonic load combination is used by secondary static model H. Power-law terms in opening area and ligament thickness predict static and fatigue safety-factor retention relative to a solid gear. The fatigue model retains exponential load mixing.

Why “power-law-based”?

The geometry penalty includes φnbR−m. The full retention expression also contains the reciprocal transformation and a load-combination rule, so it is more precise to call it a power-law-based retention model than a pure power law.
